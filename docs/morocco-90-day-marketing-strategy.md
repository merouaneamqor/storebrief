# Vazivo Morocco: 90-Day Marketing Strategy

**Purpose:** Validate demand and win the first Moroccan retail customers for Vazivo with a focused, proof-led go-to-market plan.

**Product note:** The product is **Vazivo**. Capability claims below should be verified against the live demo and plan/tenant availability.

**Planning horizon:** First 90 days after launch preparation. Targets below are proposed working targets, not forecasts or existing results.

## 1. Strategic recommendation

Position Vazivo as the operating platform that helps multi-store retailers turn head-office instructions into consistent store execution, with clear follow-up and evidence across locations.

Start with organized retailers and franchise networks that already have multiple stores, regional managers, and repeatable operating standards. Do not initially target every independent neighborhood shop: the product described in the repository is strongest in communications, campaigns, forms, checklists, org-unit management, and reporting, rather than point-of-sale or accounting.

The Moroccan Ministry of Industry and Trade identifies commerce modernization and digitalization as priorities. Its April 2026 Commerce Maroc 2030 forum also highlighted proximity-commerce modernization, merchant skills, logistics, and digital inclusion. This creates a timely opening for retail operations software, while making local fit and practical onboarding central to the pitch.

## 2. Target market and buyer

### Primary ideal customer profile

Moroccan retail groups or franchise operators with roughly 20 or more locations, a central operations team, and recurring processes that must be carried out consistently in stores. Treat the 20-store threshold as a qualification hypothesis and adjust it after discovery interviews.

Prioritize one vertical for the first pilot, selected after customer interviews:

1. Grocery, supermarket, and cash-and-carry groups
2. Fashion, beauty, and specialty retail chains
3. Quick-service restaurants and multi-site service brands

These categories are candidate segments, not confirmed prospects. Retail groups are opening and operating multiple store formats in Morocco; for example, LabelVie describes its multi-format retail business and continued store expansion. [LabelVie: Our brands](https://labelvie.ma/en/our-business/our-brands/)

### Buying group

- **Economic buyer:** COO, retail director, or general manager
- **Operational champion:** Head of store operations or regional operations
- **Daily users:** Area managers, store managers, and store employees
- **Additional reviewers:** IT, HR/training, information security, and procurement

### Best-fit trigger events

- A new store rollout or franchise expansion
- Inconsistent execution across branches
- Too much follow-up through calls, spreadsheets, and chat groups
- A seasonal promotion or audit that needs proof of completion
- A new checklist, safety, service, or merchandising standard

## 3. Positioning and message

### Positioning statement

**For retail groups managing many locations, Vazivo brings store communications, tasks, checklists, campaigns, and reporting into one operational workflow—so teams can see what was assigned, what was completed, and where follow-up is needed.**

### Core promise

**Every store knows what to do. Head office can see what happened.**

### Message pillars

1. **Reach every relevant store:** Target communications and work by store, region, or organizational group.
2. **Turn instructions into action:** Pair announcements with tasks, forms, checklists, or campaigns.
3. **See completion and evidence:** Review submissions and progress across locations.
4. **Make adoption practical:** Demonstrate a mobile-friendly workflow and validate French, Arabic, and offline requirements with real users.

### Example copy

**Headline:** Keep every store aligned, from head office to the shop floor.

**Subhead:** Send clear instructions, assign store tasks, collect responses, and follow progress across your retail network in one place.

**Call to action:** Book a store-operations demo.

Avoid claims such as “increase sales,” “eliminate missed tasks,” or “works offline everywhere” until customer pilots establish evidence and the relevant product behavior is verified.

## 4. Product and localization readiness

The repository documents communications, campaigns, forms/checklists, org-unit structures, dashboards, reports, and offline support in the forms engine. Validate the exact customer-facing behavior and plan/tenant availability before putting each capability in sales material.

Before broad promotion:

- Prepare French customer-facing pages and sales materials.
- Test Arabic translation and right-to-left layout with Moroccan users; consider Darija explanations only after interviews identify a need.
- Test forms, photo uploads, and synchronization on common mobile devices and inconsistent connections.
- Verify the actual language options, offline limits, permissions, data hosting, and security answers with product and technical teams.
- Treat WhatsApp alerts as a possible integration or follow-up channel, not an existing product feature unless confirmed.
- Do not position Vazivo as a POS, payments, inventory, or accounting product unless those functions are demonstrably available.

ANRT provides Moroccan ICT indicators in French, Arabic, and English, supporting a deliberate multilingual approach; it does not by itself establish which language each retail workforce prefers. Confirm language needs in interviews. [ANRT: Morocco ICT Data](https://www.anrt.ma/observatoires/morocco-ict-data)

## 5. Go-to-market channels

### A. Account-based selling — primary channel

Build a named list of 30–50 Moroccan retail groups and franchise operators. For each account, identify operations, regional management, and IT contacts. Lead with a short workflow-specific message, then offer a 30-minute process review and tailored demo.

Use Casablanca and Rabat as initial meeting hubs, while including groups with branches in other cities in the prospect list. Expand regional activity based on qualified demand, not assumptions about city-level market size.

### B. Partnerships and industry access

Explore introductions, workshops, or event participation through relevant retail associations, Chambers of Commerce, Industry and Services, and the Moroccan Retail Tech Builder ecosystem. The Ministry publicly describes collaboration with chambers and MRTB in commerce digitalization; this is a channel to investigate, not an existing Vazivo partnership. [Ministry: Digitalisation du Commerce](https://www.mcinet.gov.ma/fr/content/commerce-interieur/digitalisation-du-commerce)

### C. Localized digital presence

- Launch a French landing page with a concise product walkthrough and demo form.
- Add an Arabic version with genuine right-to-left design; test English as a secondary page for international leadership teams.
- Publish a short video showing one real workflow from instruction to store response to head-office review.
- Create practical French-language resources around store checklists, campaign execution, and multi-site communication. Translate the best-performing resources after demand is confirmed.

**In this repo:** the public landing at `/` already supports FR/AR (RTL) and a demo-request form.

### D. LinkedIn and retargeting

Use LinkedIn for named-account awareness and decision-maker content. Test small paid campaigns only after the landing page and demo conversion flow work. Retarget visitors with a product walkthrough or pilot offer; measure qualified meetings rather than clicks.

### E. Relationship-based follow-up

Offer meetings and demos in the prospect’s preferred language. Use email, phone, or messaging only through appropriate business contacts and with respect for consent and local requirements. Do not make an unverified WhatsApp integration a headline feature.

## 6. Lead offer and sales motion

### Entry offer: Store Execution Diagnostic

A 45-minute working session to map one process—such as a promotion rollout, store visit, opening checklist, or issue escalation—and identify how the retailer currently assigns and follows up on it. Deliver a one-page workflow summary and a proposed pilot scorecard.

### Pilot offer

Run a time-boxed pilot with one retailer, one workflow, and a manageable group of stores. A six-week pilot across approximately 5–15 locations is a starting hypothesis; adapt the scope to the customer’s operations. Agree on baseline measures before launch. Consider crediting a pilot fee toward rollout, subject to commercial validation.

### Pilot success measures

- Time from instruction sent to store completion
- Percentage of assigned stores completing by deadline
- Number of follow-up messages or calls required
- Response completeness and evidence quality
- Store-manager and area-manager adoption
- Willingness to expand to additional workflows or locations

Only publish outcome claims after the customer approves the figures and wording.

## 7. 90-day execution plan

| Period | Work | Proposed checkpoint |
|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–30: Validate** | Interview 12–15 retail leaders and store/area managers. Select one initial vertical and workflow. Audit localization and product claims. Create a French landing page, demo environment, and pilot scorecard. | 12 completed interviews; 30 qualified target accounts; one chosen pilot use case; demo materials ready. |
| **Days 31–60: Generate demand** | Begin personalized outreach to named accounts. Hold a small online or partner-led workflow session. Publish one practical guide and one product walkthrough. Run discovery calls and tailored demos. | 8–12 discovery meetings; 5–8 demos; 2–3 pilot candidates. |
| **Days 61–90: Prove value** | Start one or two pilots. Train managers and store users. Review adoption weekly, remove onboarding friction, and capture approved feedback. Present a rollout proposal based on pilot results. | 1–2 pilots launched; baseline and outcome data captured; at least one documented rollout decision. |

These are stretch planning targets for a focused team. Recalibrate after the first month based on the actual sales cycle and response rate.

## 8. Content and campaign calendar

Build content around retailers’ operating calendar rather than generic software themes. Useful campaign moments include Ramadan/Eid operations, summer trade, back-to-school, and major promotional periods; confirm exact dates and customer calendars each year.

Recommended first assets:

1. **Workflow guide:** “How to roll out a promotion consistently across every store.”
2. **Checklist pack:** Sample opening, closing, store-visit, or merchandising checks in French, with Arabic versions reviewed by local users.
3. **Short video:** Send a task, complete it in a store, review the result centrally.
4. **Pilot brief:** Scope, implementation steps, data to measure, and decision point.
5. **Customer story:** Publish only after a pilot produces verified results and the customer approves publication.

## 9. Measurement and budget

### Funnel measures

- Named accounts researched and reached
- Replies and qualified discovery meetings
- Demo-to-pilot conversion
- Pilot activation and weekly active store participation
- Pilot completion and rollout conversion
- Sales cycle length and acquisition cost once enough data exists

### First-quarter marketing effort allocation

Use this as an initial allocation of team effort, not a fixed cash budget:

- **50%:** Named-account research, outreach, and sales enablement
- **20%:** Interviews, pilot onboarding, and customer proof
- **15%:** Localized website and practical content
- **10%:** Partner and industry activity
- **5%:** Paid channel experiments

Keep paid spend limited until message-to-meeting conversion is demonstrated. Set cash budget only after confirming team capacity, event costs, and target account list.

## 10. Risks and responses

- **Localization gap:** Test French and Arabic workflows with users before making language claims.
- **Feature expectation mismatch:** Mark each capability as live, configuration-dependent, roadmap, or unavailable in all collateral.
- **Slow enterprise sales:** Use one workflow pilot and a defined decision date to reduce the first commitment.
- **Low store adoption:** Involve store managers in design, keep the workflow short, and track adoption by role and location.
- **Insufficient proof:** Sell a measurable pilot; do not invent ROI or customer references.
- **Wrong initial segment:** Conduct interviews across two or three candidate verticals, then concentrate outreach on the segment with the clearest pain and fastest pilot path.

## 11. Decisions to make before launch

1. Which Moroccan retailer segment is the first priority?
2. Which existing product workflows are available and supportable in the intended plan?
3. What French and Arabic localization is already complete?
4. What implementation and customer-success capacity can support pilots?
5. What pilot price, contract, data-hosting, and security terms can be offered?
